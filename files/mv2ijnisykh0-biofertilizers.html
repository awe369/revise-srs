Biofertilizers

Living microbes that feed the soil. One page to understand how they work, why the exam asks what it asks, and how to handle questions you haven't seen yet.

The whole chapter in one picture. Nitrogen gas is everywhere but locked by a triple bond. Only certain microbes carry the enzyme nitrogenase that can break it. A biofertilizer is simply a packet of those helpful microbes (or phosphate-releasing ones) added to seed or soil.

What a biofertilizer actually is

Biofertilizer = a culture of living or dormant microorganisms that increases nutrient availability to plants. It is not the nutrient itself — it is the worker that makes the nutrient available.

Chemical fertilisers raised yields, but overuse pollutes water, kills useful soil microbes and adds no organic matter. Biofertilizers restore the soil's own nutrient machinery and cut chemical dependence.

The three families

Bacteria

Rhizobium (in nodules), Azotobacter, Azospirillum (free-living / associative), phosphate solubilisers.

Cyanobacteria (BGA)

Nostoc, Anabaena, Oscillatoria. Fix N₂ in heterocysts. The paddy specialists, often with Azolla.

Fungi

Mycorrhiza (VAM, e.g. Glomus). Do not fix nitrogen — they bring phosphorus and water.

Sort by the nutrient, not the name. Nearly every question becomes easy once you ask: does this organism give N (fixers) or P (solubilisers/mobilisers)? Mycorrhiza is the lone P-worker in most option lists.

The oxygen paradox

Nitrogenase is poisoned by oxygen. Yet the bacteria need oxygen to make the ATP that nitrogenase burns. Every N-fixer has to solve this.

Nitrogenase is a molybdenum–iron enzyme. Its reaction is expensive:

N₂ + 8H⁺ + 8e⁻ + 16 ATP → 2 NH₃ + H₂ + 16 ADP + 16 Pi

Leghaemoglobin works like myoglobin: a haem protein that binds oxygen tightly. It keeps free O₂ low enough to protect nitrogenase, yet supplies enough for the bacteria to breathe. It makes active nodules pink/red. The plant makes it only after bacteria colonise the root (the globin part is plant-coded; the haem is linked to the bacterial partner).

How each fixer beats oxygen

OrganismSolution
Rhizobium (nodule)Leghaemoglobin mops up O₂

Trap: "Leghaemoglobin is found in human / rabbit / chicken blood" — no. It is found only in legume root nodules. Also, leghaemoglobin does not fix nitrogen itself; nitrogenase does.

Cyanobacteria and the Azolla partnership

Cyanobacteria (blue-green algae, BGA) are bacteria, not true algae. They photosynthesise and fix nitrogen, which makes them perfect for flooded rice paddies where water, light and warmth are all available. Examples: Nostoc, Anabaena, Oscillatoria, Tolypothrix, Aulosira.

Azolla + Anabaena

Azolla is a fern, not an alga or bacterium. It gives food and a home; Anabaena gives fixed nitrogen. Because the fern becomes nitrogen-rich, it is also high in protein — which is why it is grown as supplementary feed for cattle, especially milch animals, and for poultry and fish.
  • Crop: BGA and Azolla are used mainly in paddy (water-logged). Not maize, not wheat, not bajra.
  • Best combination: Azolla works best "mixed with" blue-green algae — because that is literally what it already carries.
  • BGA also add organic matter, improve soil structure and help reclaim saline–alkaline soil.
  • Other floating plants in option lists — Salvinia, Marsilea, Lemna, Wolffia, Trapa, Pteridium — are not used as biofertilizers. Salvinia and Marsilea are also ferns, which makes them good distractors.

Mycorrhiza: the phosphorus pipeline

Mycorrhiza = fungus + root. The fungus's threads (hyphae) reach far beyond the root and haul back phosphorus and water. It does not fix nitrogen.

TypeWhere the fungus sitsExamples
EctomycorrhizaSheath around root, between cellsPine, oak, eucalyptus; fungi like Pisolithus
Endomycorrhiza / VAM (arbuscular)Enters root cells, forms arbuscules and vesiclesMost crops; fungus Glomus

Benefits of mycorrhizal plants: tolerance to drought and salinity, resistance to root-borne pathogens, better overall growth. VAM is the standard answer for "phosphatic biofertilizer".

Other P-biofertilizers: fungi Aspergillus awamori, Penicillium; bacteria Bacillus megaterium var. phosphaticum, Pseudomonas striata.

Beyond the PDF: what gets asked next

Nitrogen-use efficiency (PDF Q23)

  • Split application: give urea in two or three doses rather than all at sowing, so less is lost.
  • Nitrification inhibitors: slow the conversion of NH₄⁺ to easily-leached NO₃⁻. Neem oil does this, which is why India made neem-coated urea mandatory for all domestic urea in 2015. Chemical examples: DCD, nitrapyrin.
  • Slow-release fertilisers: sulphur-coated urea, polymer-coated urea.

Legume vs non-legume (PDF Q24–26)

Nitrogen-fixing plants are legumes (family Fabaceae): gram/chickpea, pea, beans, soybean, groundnut, alfalfa, clover, pigeon pea, lentil, moong, urad, dhaincha. Not N-fixing: paddy, wheat, maize, bajra, sugarcane, amaranth, spinach, purslane (kulfa), mustard.

That's why pulses are grown in rotation with cereals, and why paddy fields rely on BGA, Azolla or urea.

Rare case: Sesbania rostrata (a dhaincha) has stem nodules made by Azorhizobium caulinodans — a classic "beyond" fact.

Compost, vermicompost and earthworms (PDF Q22, Q29)

  • Compost: biodegradable waste decomposed in pits by bacteria and fungi; earthworms speed it up.
  • Vermicompost species: Eisenia fetida (red wiggler), Eudrilus eugeniae.
  • Earthworm nicknames: "ploughers of the soil", "farmer's friend", "intestines of the earth" (Aristotle).

Biochar (PDF Q21)

Charcoal made by pyrolysis — heating crop waste or wood with little oxygen. It is porous, so it holds water and nutrients, gives microbes a habitat (including N-fixers), stores carbon for a long time, and is usable as a growing medium in vertical farming.

Policy hooks for UPSC / UPPSC

  • Biofertilizers are regulated under the Fertiliser (Control) Order, 1985, which added quality standards for them in 2006.
  • Paramparagat Krishi Vikas Yojana (PKVY), 2015 — cluster-based organic farming.
  • Soil Health Card scheme, 2015 — tells farmers which nutrients to add.
  • PM-PRANAM, 2023 — incentivises states to cut chemical fertiliser use.
  • Sikkim — declared India's first fully organic state (2016).
  • National Centre for Organic and Natural Farming, Ghaziabad — the nodal body for biofertilizer promotion.

Traps that cost marks

"Mycorrhiza fixes nitrogen." False. It is a fungus–root association that supplies phosphorus and water. In a "which is not a nitrogen-fixer" question, it is almost always the answer.
"Azolla is an alga / bacterium." False. Azolla is an aquatic fern. Its partner Anabaena is the cyanobacterium that fixes nitrogen.
"Blue-green algae are algae." They are bacteria (prokaryotes) — cyanobacteria. Red, brown and green algae do not fix nitrogen.
"Alfalfa is a biofertilizer." Alfalfa is a legume plant that fixes nitrogen with Rhizobium's help (used as fodder and green manure), but the plant itself isn't a biofertilizer culture. In PDF Q2 the answer is Azolla + BGA only.
"Azolla is used in maize." No — Azolla needs standing water, so its crop is paddy.
"Earthworms slow down composting." They speed it up.
PDF Q3 (Nitrolin, answer marked *). The book marks this question disputed. Lock in the firm facts: Azolla ✔, BGA ✔, Alfalfa ✘. Nitrolin was an old trade name for a bacterial culture, which is why some keys include it — if it recurs, choose the option with 1, 2 and 4 and without Alfalfa.
